Welcome to Grand Gorge, New York

Nestled in the northern Catskill Mountains at the intersection of State Routes 23 and 30, Grand Gorge blends over two centuries of pioneer heritage, railroad history, and small-town mountain hospitality.

Welcome to Grand Gorge

Located in the northeastern corner of Delaware County within the Town of Roxbury, Grand Gorge, New York (ZIP 12434) sits at a natural mountain pass where the East Branch of the Delaware River watershed meets the Schoharie Valley.

Known for generations as "The Crossroads of the Northern Catskills," Grand Gorge greets travelers arriving along State Route 23 and State Route 30 with classic wooden welcome signs, seasonal Main Street flower boxes, and immediate access to four-season outdoor recreation.

GGCAG – Grand Gorge Community Action Group

The Grand Gorge Community Action Group (GGCAG) was formed by dedicated Grand Gorge residents and has been responsible for initiating numerous projects that have greatly enhanced the appearance, civic pride, and welcoming spirit of the community.

Signature Community Projects

  • Main Street Flower Boxes: Hand-planted seasonal flower boxes placed along Main Street through the spring and summer months.
  • Holiday Wreaths & Lighting: Lighted Christmas wreaths mounted along the streetscape during the winter holiday season.
  • "Welcome to Grand Gorge" Gateway Signs: Custom roadside welcome signs acquired and installed at the entrances to Grand Gorge to greet residents and travelers.
  • Civic Pride & Beautification: Ongoing volunteer upkeep of public planters, seasonal banners, and community gathering spaces.
Get Involved: Community volunteers and local donors make GGCAG's year-round beautification efforts possible. Watch local bulletin boards at the Post Office and Civic Center for seasonal planting and decorating dates.

History of Grand Gorge

Originally known in early settlement days as Moresville in honor of pioneer settler John More, the community later took the name Grand Gorge—inspired by the dramatic rocky gorge carved through the surrounding Catskill peaks where mountain passes converge.

Key Milestones in Grand Gorge History

  • 1786 – First Permanent Settlement: John More and his family settled in the valley, establishing early farms and civic foundations.
  • Early 1800s – The Susquehannah Turnpike: An important east–west private toll road (today's NYS Route 23) linked the Hudson River at Catskill with the Susquehanna Valley, turning the hamlet into a vital stagecoach and drover stop.
  • 1870s – Arrival of the Railroad: Construction of the Ulster & Delaware Railroad connected Grand Gorge directly to Kingston and Oneonta, sparking growth in dairy shipping, hotels, and boarding houses.
  • 1926 – Schoharie Reservoir: Just north of the hamlet along Route 30, New York City completed the Gilboa Dam and Schoharie Reservoir, reshaping the surrounding valley landscape.
  • 1930–1980 – Grand Gorge Central School: Operated for 50 years, graduating more than 700 students whose alumni association continues to preserve local school heritage.

The Crossroads

For more than two centuries, Grand Gorge's identity has been shaped by geography. Sitting squarely at the junction of State Route 23 (east–west) and State Route 30 (north–south), the hamlet has always been a natural meeting point for travelers crossing the northern Catskills.

Whether heading west toward Stamford and Oneonta, east toward Windham and the Hudson Valley, north toward Gilboa and the Schoharie Valley, or south toward Roxbury and Margaretville, all roads pass through the heart of Grand Gorge.

Four Directions from Main Street:
• East (Rt. 23): Prattsville, Windham, Catskill & Hudson Valley
• West (Rt. 23): Stamford, Harpersfield & Oneonta
• North (Rt. 30): Schoharie Reservoir, Gilboa & Middleburgh
• South (Rt. 30): Roxbury, Arkville & Margaretville

Churches of Grand Gorge

From the earliest frontier prayer meetings held in pioneer cabins to the historic steeples along Main Street, local congregations have long anchored community life in Grand Gorge.

  • Grand Gorge United Methodist Church: Located right in the village center on Main Street, serving generations of local families with Sunday worship, community suppers, and outreach.
  • St. Philip Neri / Sacred Heart Parish: Serving Catholic residents and seasonal Catskill visitors in the Grand Gorge and Roxbury corridor.
  • Reformed & Community Heritage: Early settlers from the Hudson Valley and New England established strong Reformed and Baptist congregations that helped shape the civic character of the Town of Roxbury.

Ulster and Delaware Railroad (U&D)

The arrival of the Ulster and Delaware Railroad transformed Grand Gorge from a quiet turnpike village into a bustling Catskill Mountain hub. Running from Kingston Point on the Hudson River through the high peaks to Oneonta, the U&D line curved directly into Grand Gorge.

Life Along the Tracks

  • Grand Gorge Depot: Located off Depot Street (Route 30 South), the station handled daily passenger trains, mail pouches, freight, and express packages.
  • Dairy & Creamery Trains: Local dairy farms brought cans of fresh milk to the Grand Gorge creamery each morning for fast refrigerated rail transport to New York City markets.
  • Summer Boarding Era: City families stepped off the U&D coaches at Grand Gorge to stay at local hotels, mountain boarding houses, and farm resorts.

John More – First Settler of Grand Gorge

In 1786, John More and his family became the first permanent settlers in Grand Gorge, though their journey to the northern Catskills had begun 13 years earlier.

In the spring of 1773, John, his wife Betty (Taylor) More, and their two young sons, John T. and Robert, traveled from New York City up the Hudson Valley to Catskill and overland to Harpersfield. After enduring the hardships of the Revolutionary War frontier and waiting out the winter, the More family settled permanently in the valley that initially bore their name: Moresville.

The John More Association: Founded by descendants of John and Betty More, the association holds regular reunions in the Roxbury and Grand Gorge area, preserving one of the Catskills' oldest documented family histories.

Yesterday to Today

Today, Grand Gorge is served by two major New York State Highways: Route 23 and Route 30. In days gone by, Route 23 was a private toll road called the Susquehannah Turnpike, where travelers paid modest tolls at timber gates to cross the mountains.

While stagecoaches, tollgates, and steam locomotives have given way to modern highways, Grand Gorge retains the close-knit character of a traditional Catskill crossroads—where neighbors greet each other at the post office, local diners, and community events.
